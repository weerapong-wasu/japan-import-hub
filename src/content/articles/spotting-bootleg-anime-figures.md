The anime figure market has a counterfeit problem. Bootleg factories copy popular scale figures within months of release, and marketplace listings make fakes look identical to the real thing — until the box arrives. Here's how collectors protect themselves.

## Why bootlegs are everywhere

Popular figures sell out at Japanese retail, prices rise on the aftermarket, and counterfeiters fill the gap. The most-faked items are exactly the ones you want: hit-series scale figures, Nendoroids of flagship characters, and anything that sold out during pre-orders.

## The telltale signs of a bootleg

### Before buying

- **Price too good to be true.** A sold-out figure listed far below the aftermarket rate is the single biggest red flag.
- **Marketplace seller with mixed inventory.** Legitimate figure sellers specialize; bootleg sellers stock random everything.
- **Stock photos only.** Real resellers photograph the actual box, including the authenticity sticker where applicable.
- **"Chinese version" / "no box" listings.** These phrases are bootleg euphemisms.

### After the box arrives

| Check | Genuine | Bootleg |
|---|---|---|
| Paint edges | Crisp, clean lines | Bleed, smudges, missed spots |
| Skin tones | Subtle gradients | Flat or oddly orange |
| Plastic feel | Solid, weighty | Light, oily surface |
| Box print | Sharp text, correct logos | Blurry print, wrong fonts |
| Base fit | Pegs align perfectly | Loose or forced fit |
| Manufacturer sticker | Present where applicable | Missing or fake holograms |

## The one rule that avoids the entire problem

**Buy at pre-order, from official channels.** Bootlegs exist because people chase sold-out figures on open marketplaces. If you pre-order from a Japanese retailer or established exporter during the pre-order window, counterfeits are simply not in the supply chain.

Japanese figure retail runs on a predictable cycle: announcement → pre-order window (weeks to months) → release → sellout. Get into the habit of pre-ordering the moment a figure you love opens reservations, and the bootleg question never touches you.

## If you missed the pre-order

You still have safe options, in order of preference:

1. **Official re-releases.** Popular figures get second production runs — watch for re-release announcements.
2. **Established import stores' remaining stock.** Exporters like Play-Asia list official stock clearly and source from authorized distribution.
3. **Reputable specialist resellers** with photo verification and authenticity guarantees.
4. **Auction sites** — only with seller history research and genuine-photo verification. This is expert territory.

## Protecting resale value

Authentic figures hold value; bootlegs are worthless. Keep the box, keep the blister packaging, and keep any authenticity stickers intact. A complete-in-box genuine figure from a sold-out run is one of the few collectibles that routinely appreciates — a bootleg is landfill with paint.

Collect slowly, pre-order early, buy official. Your shelf — and your wallet — will thank you.
