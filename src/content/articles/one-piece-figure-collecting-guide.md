One Piece has been serialized since 1997 and is the best-selling manga in history — which means its figure ecosystem has had nearly three decades to grow into one of the deepest, most collected, and most counterfeited in the world. For a collector, that combination means one thing: the rewards are huge, and so is the value of knowing what you're doing before you spend.

## The One Piece figure landscape

| Line | Maker | What it is | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portrait.Of.Pirates (P.O.P) | MegaHouse | The prestige scale-figure line, running since 2004 | Premium |
| Figuarts ZERO | Bandai | Fixed-pose statues, dramatic "Extra Battle" scenes | Mid-premium |
| S.H.Figuarts | Bandai | Articulated, swappable parts | Premium articulated |
| Ichiban Kuji | Bandai Spirits | Lottery prize statues, themed waves | Mid, chase-driven |
| Grandline Series / DXF | Banpresto | Prize figures, huge variety | Budget |
| World Collectable (WCF) | Banpresto | Chibi mini-figures, collected in sets | Entry |

Two lines deserve special mention. **P.O.P is the line serious One Piece collectors build lives around** — long-running, consistently excellent sculpts, and older releases become genuinely hard to find. **World Collectable Figures are the opposite entry point**: tiny, affordable, and dangerously collectible in complete sets.

## Why importing matters more for One Piece than almost any franchise

The sheer size of the fandom means western retail only ever carries a thin slice of what Japan produces. Ichiban Kuji waves are designed for Japanese lottery shops and reach overseas fans mainly through exporters and aftermarket channels. P.O.P releases sell out at Japanese pre-order and command steep aftermarket prices later. If you collect One Piece seriously, importing isn't a nice-to-have — it's the supply chain.

The dark side of a giant fandom: One Piece sits alongside Dragon Ball at the top of the world's most-bootlegged franchises. Fake P.O.P figures are an entire industry. The protection is the same as ever — pre-order from official channels, and run any aftermarket listing through the checks in our [bootleg-spotting guide](/guides/spotting-bootleg-anime-figures) before money moves.

## The pre-order discipline, One Piece edition

P.O.P and Figuarts ZERO releases follow the standard Japanese cycle: announcement → pre-order window → release → sellout. With a cast this large, the temptation is to chase everything — the collectors who stay happy (and solvent) pick a crew member or an era and let the rest sail past.

A sane structure that many longtime collectors converge on: one P.O.P of your favorite character as the centerpiece, a handful of prize figures for shelf depth, and a WCF set for the fun of completion. Total outlay stays reasonable, and every piece is official.

## Counting the real cost

One Piece figures range from pocket-money WCF minis to P.O.P pieces that cost as much as a console. ## The long game

Here's the evergreen truth about One Piece: the story is heading toward its finale era, and franchises historically see collecting interest *intensify* around and after their conclusions — the works become complete, canonical, and nostalgic all at once. A collection built now, from official channels, with provenance you trust, is positioned beautifully for the decades of fandom still ahead. Set sail properly: official channels, pre-orders, real math.
