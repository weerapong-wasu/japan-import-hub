Gundam has been on air since 1979, and Gunpla — Gundam plastic models — have been in continuous production nearly as long. That makes Gunpla one of the most reliable evergreen hobbies you can enter: the kits never stop coming, the skills you build compound, and a franchise with four decades of momentum isn't going anywhere. Here's how importing fits into it.

## Gunpla grades in one table

The grade system tells you size, complexity, and price tier before you read anything else:

| Grade | Scale | Difficulty | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| EG (Entry Grade) | 1/144 | Beginner — no tools needed | First kit ever |
| HG (High Grade) | 1/144 | Easy–moderate | The backbone of most collections |
| RG (Real Grade) | 1/144 | Detailed, fiddly | Detail lovers who want small kits |
| MG (Master Grade) | 1/100 | Moderate–advanced | The serious builder's staple |
| PG (Perfect Grade) | 1/60 | Advanced, long builds | Milestone projects |
| SD | Non-scale | Easy | Fun, stylized, quick builds |

A first-time builder is best served by an EG or HG — they snap together without glue, and modern engineering means even the cheap kits articulate beautifully.

## Why import Gunpla at all?

Three reasons that have stayed true for decades:

**P-Bandai exclusives.** Bandai's premium web shop releases kits — recolors, variant units, side-story mobile suits — that are never sold in regular retail, anywhere. Japanese exporters and proxy services are how overseas builders get them. If a kit you want says "P-Bandai," importing isn't optional; it's the only route.

**Selection depth.** Local hobby shops stock the popular kits. Japan's supply chain stocks *everything currently in production* — including older HGs from series you grew up with that western retail quietly dropped years ago.

**Restock reality.** Gunpla sells out globally in waves, but Japanese channels get restocks first and deepest. When a kit vanishes from your local shelves for a year, it's often quietly back in stock in Japan.

## The good news about bootlegs

Unlike figures, Gunpla has a relatively manageable counterfeit situation — fake kits exist but are easier to spot (off-brand boxes, wrong logos) and mostly live on sketchy marketplaces. Buy from established Japanese exporters or hobby retailers and the problem essentially doesn't exist. The same one-rule principle from our [bootleg guide](/guides/spotting-bootleg-anime-figures) applies: official channel, no worries.

## Shipping math: the one Gunpla-specific quirk

Gunpla boxes are **big and light** — which means international shipping is priced on box volume (dimensional weight), not actual weight. A single PG box can cost more to ship than five HG kits packed together. Two habits keep costs sane:

1. **Batch your orders.** Three or four HG kits in one shipment costs far less per kit than shipping them separately.
2. **Run the total first.** Use our [Landed Cost Calculator](/calculator) with the item subtotal and the shipping quote before you commit — big-box kits are exactly where surprise totals happen.

## A sensible import routine for builders

Start with a batch of HGs from series you love — enough to fill one shipment efficiently. Build them, learn what grades suit your patience level, then graduate to MG. Keep an eye on P-Bandai announcements for your favorite series, because those exclusives are the pieces that define a collection years later — and they never come back once the order window closes.

Gunpla rewards the long game: skills stack, tools last, and the franchise's 45-year track record says the kits you build today will have company for decades. Few hobbies are this genuinely evergreen — import smart and enjoy the build.
