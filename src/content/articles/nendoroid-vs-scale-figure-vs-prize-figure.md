Every figure listing throws around the same words — Nendoroid, 1/7 scale, prize figure, Pop Up Parade — like you already know what they mean. You don't need to guess. Each term describes a completely different manufacturing tier, price range, and quality level, and knowing the difference before you buy is the single fastest way to avoid disappointment.

## The three tiers, at a glance

| Type | Typical price | Height | Articulation | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nendoroid | ¥5,000–8,000 | ~10cm | High (swappable faces/parts) | Cute display, playing with poses, budget collecting |
| Scale figure (1/7, 1/8) | ¥15,000–30,000+ | 20–30cm | None (fixed pose) | Display centerpieces, high detail, long-term value |
| Prize figure | ¥2,500–6,000 | 15–25cm | None (fixed pose) | Casual fans, trying a character before committing to scale |

## Nendoroids: cute, poseable, collectible

Made by Good Smile Company, Nendoroids are the chibi-style figures with the big heads and interchangeable face plates. They come with accessories — extra hands, props, sometimes swappable outfit parts — so you can restyle the same figure into different scenes.

**Why people love them:** they're affordable enough to collect an entire cast, poseable enough to photograph creatively, and small enough that even a large collection doesn't need a display cabinet.

**What to watch for:** popular characters sell out at pre-order and get re-released later, sometimes at a markup on secondary listings. If you see a Nendoroid at a steep discount from an unfamiliar seller, treat it like any other [potential bootleg](/guides/spotting-bootleg-anime-figures) and check seller legitimacy first.

## Scale figures: the serious collector's tier

Scale figures (usually 1/7 or 1/8 scale, sometimes 1/4 for premium lines) are sculpted with fixed poses, painted by hand-finishing processes, and priced accordingly. This is where you'll find the dramatic action poses, flowing fabric sculpting, and diorama-style bases that make a shelf look like a gallery.

Because they're expensive and often limited-run, scale figures are also the most heavily counterfeited category. The pre-order-only rule matters most here — see our [bootleg-spotting guide](/guides/spotting-bootleg-anime-figures) before you commit real money to an aftermarket listing.

**Rule of thumb:** if a figure costs more than a full-price video game, it's a scale figure, and it deserves the same due diligence you'd give any collectible investment.

## Prize figures: casual, common, and completely legitimate

Don't let the word "prize" fool you — these aren't cheap knockoffs. Prize figures (SEGA Luminasta, Banpresto, Furyu) are officially licensed figures originally distributed through Japanese crane game machines, now sold directly by import retailers in huge variety.

They won't match a scale figure's paint detail or articulation, but for the price, they're an excellent way to own a character you love without a three-figure price tag. Lines like Pop Up Parade sit in a similar affordable-but-official space, aimed squarely at collectors who want quality without the scale-figure commitment.

## Which one should you actually buy?

- **New to figure collecting, tight budget?** Start with Nendoroids or Pop Up Parade — official, affordable, easy to display.
- **Want one showpiece figure for a favorite character?** Save for a scale figure and pre-order it the moment it's announced.
- **Just want variety without spending much?** Prize figures let you build a fun, casual collection fast.
