Some manga end and slowly fade. *Slam Dunk* ended its serialization in 1996 and somehow kept growing — three decades of new readers, perpetual "greatest sports manga ever" rankings across Asia, and a 2022 film that broke box-office records in Japan, China, and Southeast Asia and introduced Shohoku to yet another generation. For collectors, that's the profile of a franchise whose artifacts don't age out. Here's how to collect it from the source.

## The manga: know your editions

Japanese manga collecting is edition-driven, and Slam Dunk is the perfect case study because it exists in several distinct formats:

| Edition | What it is | Why collectors want it |
|---|---|---|
| Original tankōbon (31 vols) | The classic 90s release | The nostalgic artifact — the spines fans grew up seeing |
| Kanzenban (24 vols) | Larger format, color pages restored | The premium reading experience |
| Shinsōban (20 vols) | Re-release with new Inoue cover art | Newer covers, modern print quality |

There's no wrong answer — but know which one a listing is selling before you buy, because sellers mix them up and prices differ meaningfully. Japanese-language editions are the collectible originals; if you just want to *read* it, licensed translations exist locally and cost far less than importing.

Related treasures for the deep end: Inoue's art books, and the famous "10 Days After" — the ending Inoue drew on chalkboards in an abandoned school, later published in Japan.

## Figures: a field reborn

For years Slam Dunk figures were scarce. The film era changed everything — a wave of officially licensed scale figures and sets of the Shohoku starting five appeared, and the franchise now has a live, ongoing figure market again.

Collector notes specific to Slam Dunk:

- **Team sets are the signature format.** Shohoku's starting five displayed together is *the* Slam Dunk shelf — which means completing a set matters more here than in most franchises, and missing one member stings.
- **Film-era items follow film-era cycles.** Merchandise waves tied to the movie sold out fast and now circulate on the aftermarket. As always, sold-out popularity attracts bootlegs — the checks in our [bootleg-spotting guide](/guides/spotting-bootleg-anime-figures) apply in full.
- **Prize figures fill the budget tier.** Affordable, officially licensed, and a good way to start a Shohoku shelf without scale-figure prices.

## Why Slam Dunk works as an "evergreen" collection

Basketball itself keeps the story permanently relevant — every new season of real-world basketball mints new fans who eventually find the greatest basketball story ever drawn. The manga's themes (growth, teamwork, the beauty of losing well) never date. And Inoue's art has only risen in critical stature. Collections built on franchises like this don't depend on a current anime season to hold meaning or value.

## Importing it right

Manga is heavy — book shipments are where sea/surface shipping genuinely shines, cutting costs dramatically if you can wait a few weeks. Figures follow the usual rules: pre-order new releases from official channels, scrutinize aftermarket listings for anything sold out. *Slam Dunk* is proof that a finished story can keep growing forever. A collection built around it carries the same property: complete, classic, and never out of style.
