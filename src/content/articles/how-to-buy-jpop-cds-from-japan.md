Japan is the world's last great physical music market. CDs aren't nostalgia there — they're the primary collector format, loaded with photobooks, DVDs, event codes, and packaging that streaming will never replicate. Buying direct from Japan is easier than most fans realize.

## Why buy direct instead of local resellers?

Three reasons:

1. **Price.** Local anime shops and marketplace resellers typically charge a substantial markup over the Japanese retail price. Direct import, even with shipping, is usually cheaper.
2. **Editions.** Resellers stock one version. Japanese stores list *every* edition — and J-pop releases often ship in three or more.
3. **First-press bonuses.** These vanish from reseller channels almost immediately, but direct stores clearly mark availability.

## Understanding Japanese CD editions

A typical major-artist single releases in multiple simultaneous versions:

| Edition | What it usually contains |
|---|---|
| 通常盤 (Regular) | CD only, cheapest, sometimes a unique B-side |
| 初回限定盤 (First Press Limited) | CD + DVD/Blu-ray, photobook, special packaging |
| Type A / B / C | Different bonus discs or member covers (idol groups) |
| 完全生産限定盤 (Complete Production Limited) | One print run, never repressed — the true collector item |

**First press (初回) matters.** Even the "regular" edition often has a first-press variant with an extra — a poster, a card, a serial code. Stores mark whether current stock is first press, and this status can change day to day as old stock sells through.

## Placing your order, step by step

1. **Search in romaji.** English-friendly stores index artists in Latin script — "Yoasobi" works fine, no Japanese input needed.
2. **Pick your edition carefully.** Check the tracklist and bonus contents listed on the product page. Type A and Type B may have entirely different B-sides.
3. **Watch the release date.** Pre-orders lock in first-press stock. Ordering pre-release is the only way to *guarantee* first-press extras.
4. **Choose shipping by urgency.** Lightweight CDs ship affordably by registered airmail; boxsets justify courier services.
5. **Combine orders.** Shipping per-item drops sharply when you bundle several CDs into one parcel. Most importers batch monthly.

## The bonus (特典) system

Japanese music retail runs on bonuses. Two kinds matter to importers:

- **First-press bonuses (初回特典)** — included inside the packaging by the label. Anyone who buys early gets them.
- **Store bonuses (店舗特典)** — exclusive to specific retailers: a photocard from one shop, a poster from another. Export stores participate in this system, and listings state which bonus their stock carries.

If a listing says nothing about bonuses, assume none — and if you're chasing a specific photocard, confirm before ordering.

## What about event codes and lottery tickets?

Some CDs include serial codes for handshake events, online meets, or ticket lotteries. Be aware: these events are almost always Japan-only, and codes often expire within weeks. Don't pay a premium for a code you can't use.

## Your first order

Start with one artist and one release you actually love. The package that arrives — obi strip intact, booklet thick as a magazine, that unmistakable Japanese print quality — will explain physical collecting better than any article can.
