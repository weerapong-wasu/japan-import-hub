City pop's global revival turned Japanese vinyl from a niche into a movement. Original 1980s pressings now command serious prices — but the reissue wave means starting a Japanese vinyl collection has never been more accessible.

## Why Japanese pressings are special

Japanese vinyl earned its reputation decades ago and never lost it:

- **Pressing quality.** Japanese plants were famous for quiet vinyl, tight quality control, and heavier-than-spec pressings.
- **The obi strip.** Like CDs, Japanese LPs carry an obi — and for vintage records, obi presence can multiply the value.
- **Inserts done right.** Lyric sheets, posters, and liner notes are consistently more elaborate than Western equivalents.

## The city pop entry points

If you're arriving via the city pop wave, know the two paths:

**Original pressings** of the classic era are auction-market territory now: condition-graded, competitive, and priced accordingly. Thrilling, but not a beginner's first move.

**Official reissues** are the smart entry. Japanese labels have been steadily reissuing the classic catalog — remastered, on fresh vinyl, at normal retail prices, often with the original artwork and obi reproduced. New reissues are announced constantly, and pre-ordering from a Japanese store is dramatically cheaper than buying the same reissue after Western hype discovers it.

> Collector's rule: the reissue you pre-order today at retail price is the "rare pressing" someone else pays triple for in two years.

## Modern J-pop and anime vinyl

Vinyl isn't just retro in Japan anymore. Current artists release limited LP editions, and anime soundtracks — especially prestige series scores — get gorgeous pressings with gatefold art. These runs are *small*. The limited edition dynamics from the CD world apply doubly to vinyl: pre-order or pay aftermarket prices.

## Practical shipping notes

Vinyl changes the import calculus compared to CDs:

1. **Weight.** LPs are heavy; shipping cost per record drops significantly when combining 2–4 records per order.
2. **Packaging.** Established Japanese exporters know how to pack records — stiffeners, corner protection — better than most marketplace sellers.
3. **Seam splits.** Ask any collector: proper packing matters more than speed. Choose the sturdier shipping option over the fastest one.

## Condition language for pre-owned hunting

When you graduate to Japan's legendary second-hand market, learn the grading shorthand: Japanese sellers grade conservatively, which is why "VG+" from Japan routinely arrives looking like another market's "NM". Pre-owned Japanese vinyl is one of collecting's genuine bargains for exactly this reason.

## Building your first shelf

Start with three records: one city pop reissue for the history, one modern release from an artist you follow, and one anime score for the artwork. That trio will teach you editions, shipping, and the particular joy of sliding an obi-wrapped LP out of a perfectly packed box from Tokyo.
