Open any Japanese artist's release page and you'll find four versions of the same single. New collectors freeze here. This guide decodes the system permanently.

## The core vocabulary

| Japanese | Romaji | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 通常盤 | tsuujouban | Regular edition |
| 初回盤 / 初回限定盤 | shokaiban / shokai genteiban | First press / first press limited |
| 限定盤 | genteiban | Limited edition |
| 完全生産限定盤 | kanzen seisan genteiban | Complete production limited (never repressed) |
| 特典 | tokuten | Bonus item |
| 帯 | obi | The paper spine strip — keep it, collectors care |

## The edition pyramid

Think of Japanese releases as a pyramid of scarcity:

**Complete Production Limited (完全生産限定盤)** sits at the top. One manufacturing run, decided before release. When it sells out, it's gone forever. These hold value best and are the priority pre-order.

**First Press Limited (初回限定盤)** is next. Produced only in the initial run, typically with a bonus disc or photobook and special packaging. Often still available at release week, rarely months later.

**First press of the regular edition** is subtle: the same regular CD, but early copies include an extra (poster, card, code) that later pressings drop. Stores mark this as "first press included" while stock lasts.

**Regular edition (通常盤)** is always available and sometimes has its own exclusive B-side — occasionally making it, ironically, the version completists also need.

## Type A / B / C: the idol multiplier

Idol and large-group releases multiply editions by "Type": Type A with one music video, Type B with another, Type C with different B-sides, member-specific covers, and so on. This is deliberate — fans buy multiple copies — but importers on a budget should check the tracklists and pick the Type whose content they actually want, not the letter that sounds primary.

## Which should you buy?

A simple decision rule:

- **You love the artist** → First Press Limited. The photobook and packaging are the point of physical media.
- **You want the rarest** → Complete Production Limited, pre-ordered the day it's announced.
- **You want the songs** → Regular edition, and check whether it has an exclusive B-side.
- **You're chasing a specific bonus** → Confirm the exact 特典 in the listing before ordering. Never assume.

## The pre-order rule

Every rule above compresses into one habit: **pre-order before release day.** First press means *first* — the initial manufacturing run. Pre-ordering is the only guaranteed way in. After release, first-press stock becomes a countdown timer, and "still first press?" becomes a gamble that listings resolve day by day.

## Keep the obi

One last collector habit: never throw away the obi strip. A Japanese CD without its obi loses meaningful collector value — like a first-edition book missing its dust jacket. Store it in the case, behind the tray if needed, and future-you (or a future buyer) will be grateful.
