The number on the product page is almost never what leaves your card. Between international shipping and customs, a figure that "costs" ¥12,000 can easily land at 40–60% more than that once it actually arrives at your door. Here's what actually goes into that final number, with real ranges.

## The four things that make up your real total

### 1. Item price (the easy part)

This is the yen price on the store listing. Straightforward — but remember it's in yen, and exchange rates move daily. A figure that felt like a bargain when you bookmarked it can quietly get more expensive by the time you check out weeks later at pre-order confirmation.

### 2. International shipping

This is where budgets go sideways. A single boxed scale figure, well-packed to survive transit, typically runs:

| Shipping method | Typical cost (single figure) | Speed |
|---|---|---|
| Surface / economy mail | $8–15 | 3–6 weeks |
| Standard airmail | $15–30 | 1–2 weeks |
| Express (EMS/courier) | $30–60+ | 3–7 days |

Bigger figures (large scale, dioramas with bases) cost noticeably more to ship due to box dimensions, not just weight — retailers calculate "dimensional weight," so an oversized-but-light box can cost as much to ship as a small-but-heavy one.

### 3. Customs duty and import tax

This is the part first-time importers forget entirely. Most countries charge duty and/or VAT/GST on imported goods once the shipment's declared value crosses a threshold — and that threshold, and the resulting rate, is different in every country. Some countries apply nothing under a certain value; others tax from the first dollar.

**We can't tell you your exact rate here** — it depends entirely on where you live — but you should look up your country's de minimis (duty-free) threshold before ordering anything expensive, so a "great deal" doesn't turn into a surprise customs bill on delivery.

### 4. Payment and currency conversion fees

Your card issuer or payment processor typically adds a foreign transaction fee (commonly 1–3%) on top of the exchange rate itself. It's small individually, but on a $150 order it's real money, and it compounds if you're a frequent importer.

## A worked example

Say you're importing a mid-range 1/7 scale figure listed at ¥18,000, shipped via standard airmail, with your country charging an estimated 7% import duty:

| Line item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Item price | ¥18,000 |
| Shipping (standard airmail) | ¥3,500 |
| Subtotal | ¥21,500 |
| Estimated duty (7%) | ¥1,505 |
| **Total landed cost** | **¥23,005 (≈ $154 USD)** |

That's roughly 28% above the sticker price — and this example didn't even include express shipping or a high-duty country. ## How to keep the real cost down

- **Batch your orders.** Combining multiple items into one shipment splits the fixed shipping cost across more items, and some retailers offer combined-shipping discounts.
- **Choose surface shipping when you're not in a rush.** The savings versus express are substantial for non-urgent orders.
- **Check your country's duty-free threshold** and, where legal and accurate, keep individual order values under it when it makes sense to.
- **Pre-order instead of buying aftermarket.** Aftermarket sellers price in their own margin on top of everything above — pre-order pricing is close to the true landed cost.

Knowing your real total before you click "buy" is the difference between a hobby you enjoy and one that quietly resents your bank statement. Run the numbers first — it takes fifteen seconds.
