If you've spent any time in retro-import or rare-figure communities, you've seen the word "proxy" thrown around like everyone already knows what it means. A proxy shopping service is a company that buys something on a Japan-only marketplace on your behalf, receives it at a Japanese address, and then ships it to you internationally. It sounds complicated because, for a lot of purchases, it's more than you need.

## What a proxy service actually does

Sites like Yahoo Auctions Japan, Mercari Japan, and many small Japanese hobby shops don't accept international cards, don't ship overseas, and sometimes don't even have an English interface. A proxy service — Buyee, Zenmarket, and similar — solves this by:

1. Letting you browse and request items in English
2. Purchasing the item in yen with a Japanese payment method
3. Receiving the package at their Japan-based warehouse
4. Repacking (sometimes combining multiple items) and shipping it internationally to you

You pay the item price, plus the platform's service fee, plus international shipping from their warehouse.

## When you genuinely need one

- **The item is only listed on Yahoo Auctions Japan or Mercari.** This is common for out-of-print retro games, used figures, and one-off collector items that never had a Western release or export listing.
- **You're buying secondhand from an individual seller**, not a retailer — auctions and consumer-to-consumer marketplaces are proxy territory almost by definition.
- **You want to combine many small purchases into one international shipment** to save on shipping — proxies are genuinely excellent at this if you're buying from multiple small Japanese sellers at once.

## When you don't need one (and should skip it)

- **The item is in stock at Play-Asia, CDJapan, or a similar direct exporter.** These retailers already ship internationally with English checkout — a proxy would just add an unnecessary fee layer on top of a purchase that was already simple.
- **You're buying something new and current**, not rare or discontinued. New releases are almost always available through direct exporters.
- **You're new to importing.** Proxies add real complexity — warehouse holding periods, repacking requests, an extra company to communicate with if something goes wrong. Get comfortable with direct retailer imports first; reach for a proxy only once you hit something a direct store simply doesn't carry.

## The real cost difference

Proxy orders typically add a service fee (often a flat fee or a percentage of item price, varies by platform) on top of the item price and shipping — and that's before you consider that Yahoo Auctions items are often used, meaning condition risk a retailer purchase doesn't carry. For anything a direct exporter stocks, the proxy route is almost always more expensive and slower, not less.

Before assuming you need a proxy, check whether [Play-Asia](/games) or [CDJapan](/music) already carries the item directly — for the large majority of games, CDs, and current-run figures, they do, and skipping the proxy layer saves both money and hassle.

## If you do go the proxy route

Run your total cost through our [Landed Cost Calculator](/calculator) once you know the item price and estimated shipping — proxy fees stack with duty and shipping the same way a direct purchase does, and it's easy to underestimate the final number when there's an extra fee layer involved.

The short version: proxies are a genuinely useful tool for the specific job of reaching Japan-only auction and marketplace listings — not a default first step for every import. Use a direct retailer whenever one carries what you want, and save the proxy for when you actually need it.
