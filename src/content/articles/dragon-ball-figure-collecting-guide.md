Dragon Ball has been running since before many of its collectors were born, and that's exactly why it's one of the best franchises to collect: the fanbase spans three generations, the figure lines never stop, and the demand for authentic pieces never fades. It's also why Dragon Ball is one of the *most bootlegged franchises on Earth* — which makes knowing how to import properly worth real money.

## The main Dragon Ball figure lines, decoded

Walk into the Dragon Ball figure world and you'll meet the same line names again and again. Here's what they actually mean:

| Line | Maker | Style | Typical tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| S.H.Figuarts | Bandai | Highly articulated, swappable faces/hands | Premium collectible |
| Figure-rise Standard | Bandai | Build-it-yourself model kits | Affordable, hands-on |
| Ichiban Kuji | Bandai Spirits | Lottery-prize statues, striking poses | Mid-tier, chase items |
| Grandista / Blood of Saiyans | Banpresto | Large prize figures | Budget-friendly |
| Dragon Stars | Bandai (Western line) | Articulated, wider retail | Entry level |

If you're new, the practical difference is this: **S.H.Figuarts is where serious collecting happens** (and where bootlegs concentrate), **prize figures are the affordable joy**, and **Figure-rise kits are the sweet spot** if you enjoyed building things as a kid — they scratch the same itch as Gunpla.

## Why Dragon Ball collectors import

Two reasons. First, **Japan gets exclusives** — web-exclusive S.H.Figuarts releases, event-limited colorways, and Ichiban Kuji prize tiers that simply never leave Japan through official channels. Second, **authenticity**: buying from Japanese retail supply chains means the bootleg question largely disappears, because fakes enter the market through open marketplaces, not through official exporters.

That second point matters more for Dragon Ball than almost any other franchise. Popular Figuarts releases get counterfeited within months, and the fakes have gotten good — convincing box prints, similar articulation, close-enough paint. Our [bootleg-spotting guide](/guides/spotting-bootleg-anime-figures) applies to every franchise, but Dragon Ball collectors should treat it as required reading.

## The pre-order habit that defines successful DBZ collecting

Dragon Ball Figuarts follow a predictable rhythm: announcement, pre-order window, release, sellout, aftermarket markup. The collectors who own full lineups without ever overpaying are simply the ones who **pre-order during the window** instead of chasing sold-out stock later.

A practical routine that takes five minutes a week: check the announcement cycles (Bandai reveals new Figuarts monthly), and when a character you care about opens pre-orders, order it then. Waiting "to see if you really want it" is how you end up paying double to a reseller — or worse, buying a fake.

## What it really costs

A standard S.H.Figuarts release lands in a predictable range once you add international shipping, and prize figures are lighter and cheaper to ship than you'd expect since they're mostly hollow PVC. Before committing to any order, run the numbers through our [Landed Cost Calculator](/calculator) — item plus shipping plus your country's duty, converted to your currency. Dragon Ball collecting stays fun when there are no surprise costs at the door.

## Starting your collection: a sane path

1. **Pick your Saiyan (or villain).** Character-focused collections stay affordable and look intentional; "everything" collections stall out fast.
2. **Start with one premium piece** — a Figuarts of your favorite character — plus a couple of prize figures for shelf variety.
3. **Pre-order anything you love from announcement day.** This single habit eliminates bootleg risk and aftermarket markups simultaneously.
4. **Track your landed costs** so the hobby stays a joy, not a budget mystery.

Dragon Ball has outlived every trend cycle since the 1980s. Collect it the patient way — official channels, pre-orders, real math — and your shelf will still be worth something when the next generation discovers Goku all over again.
